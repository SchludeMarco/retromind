Privacy Policy

Applies to RetroMind – Time Travel (retromind.vercel.app) and RetroMind – Gaming (retromind-gaming.vercel.app). Last updated: October 8, 2026

In short

  • No tracking, no third-party analytics tools, no ads, no cookies set by RetroMind itself. The only thing counted is, anonymously, how often the apps are opened (see section 10).
  • Your journey, your profile and your settings stay in your browser on your device.
  • Fonts are hosted on our own server. Our server fetches pictures, sounds and text from Wikipedia/Wikimedia for you, so your browser doesn’t connect to third-party servers for them.
  • Spotify (music) and YouTube (videos) only load once you agree. You only use Google sign-in, AI features and feedback if you trigger them yourself.

1. Controller

The controller within the meaning of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is the operator named in the Legal Notice. There you’ll also find the contact details for any questions about privacy.

2. Hosting and server log files

RetroMind is hosted by Vercel Inc., 440 N Barranca Ave #4133, Covina, CA 91723, USA. When you open the site, Vercel processes technically necessary data: IP address, date and time, the address requested, browser and operating system (user agent), and the amount of data transferred. This is necessary to deliver the site and operate it securely (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Vercel only keeps these logs for a short time. Vercel is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework; in addition, the EU Standard Contractual Clauses apply (Vercel’s Data Processing Addendum).

3. Storage in your browser

RetroMind stores what you enter in your browser’s local storage (localStorage), not on our server: in Time Travel, for example, your name, date of birth, interests, memories, photos and diary; in the Gaming edition, your high score, collection, achievements, coins and found Easter eggs; and in both, settings such as sound, text size, color scheme and your privacy choices. The language you choose is also stored locally in your browser. This storage is strictly necessary for the features you ask for (Section 25(2) No. 2 of the German Telecommunications Digital Services Data Protection Act (TDDDG), Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR). You can delete everything at any time: in Time Travel via “Start over” on the start page, or for both editions by clearing the site data in your browser.

4. AI features (Google Gemini)

Questions, answers, the chat buddy, photo analysis and short videos in Time Travel, as well as the Retro Guru and the game guides in the Gaming edition, are generated by Google’s Gemini AI. Only when you use one of these features does our server send the content needed for it to Google’s Gemini API (Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland; processing also by Google LLC, USA): that is, your text and answers, a photo you upload for it, and details such as the decade or game title. Your browser doesn’t connect to Google itself, and your IP address is not passed on. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (the feature you requested). For game guides, Gemini uses Google Search; the search suggestions shown only lead to Google when you click them. Google LLC is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework. Please don’t enter sensitive data about other people into the AI.

5. Sign-in with Google and backup to Google Drive (optional)

You can choose to sign in with your Google account to back up your journey or your save game to your own Google Drive. Your browser only loads Google’s sign-in script (Google Identity Services) once you reach for the sign-in button. After you sign in, we receive your name, email address, profile picture and, if you allow it, your date of birth and gender from Google. Time Travel only uses this information so it doesn’t have to ask you for it separately; it stays on your device (and in your own Drive backup) and can be changed in the settings at any time. The backup is kept in a private app folder in your Google Drive that only RetroMind can see; none of it is stored on our server. A sign-in token stays in your browser for no more than one hour. The legal basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, Section 25(1) TDDDG). You can sign out at any time and revoke access in your Google account under “Third-party apps and services”. Provider: Google Ireland Limited (address see above).

6. Music from Spotify (Time Travel and Gaming edition, only with consent)

Time Travel can play the hits of each decade through Spotify’s embedded player (Spotify AB, Regeringsgatan 19, 111 53 Stockholm, Sweden), and the Gaming edition can play a playlist of ’80s heavy metal in the arcade. The player only loads once you choose “Allow music” (Time Travel) or “Allow metal” (Gaming edition); each address asks separately. Spotify then receives, among other things, your IP address and device information and may set cookies, including to recognize you if you’re signed in to Spotify. Optionally, you can also sign in with your Spotify account (Spotify then receives profile data and your email; we only store a sign-in token in your browser). Signed in with Spotify Premium, you hear a short clip of the song playing inside in front of the gaming hall; our server fetches this clip from Spotify (or, if there is none, from Deezer), your browser doesn’t contact these services for it, and nothing but the song is sent. The legal basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, Section 25(1) TDDDG). You can withdraw it at any time in the settings (⚙️) (“Stop loading Spotify”). Spotify’s privacy policy: spotify.com/de/legal/privacy-policy.

7. Videos from YouTube (Gaming edition, only with consent)

The Gaming edition shows matching videos from YouTube (Google Ireland Limited, address see above). Our server asks YouTube which videos match; only the game title is sent for this, and our server also fetches the thumbnails. The video player itself only loads once you choose “Allow YouTube”. We use the privacy-enhanced mode (youtube-nocookie.com). Even so, YouTube receives your IP address and device information when a video plays and may store data in your browser. The legal basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, Section 25(1) TDDDG); you can withdraw it at any time in the settings under “Privacy”. Google’s privacy policy: policies.google.com/privacy.

8. Content from Wikipedia and other pictures and sounds

Our server loads text and pictures from Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons, YouTube thumbnails, sound effects and background patterns from the respective source and passes them on to you. Your browser has no contact with these providers, and our server doesn’t pass on any data about you. Fonts are built directly into RetroMind; there is no connection to Google Fonts.

9. Links to other sites

RetroMind links to other sites (e.g. Wikipedia, YouTube, Spotify, GameFAQs, MobyGames, Reddit, archive.org). Only when you click such a link does your browser connect to the respective provider; that provider’s privacy policy applies there.

10. Anonymous usage statistics

So that the operator can see how many people use RetroMind, our server counts how often Time Travel and the Gaming edition are opened. For this, the app sends the name of the app (“zeitreise” or “gaming”), the type of device (phone, tablet or computer, derived from the browser identifier) and whether the app is running as an installed app to our server on launch. It also counts which decades are opened in Time Travel and which games and mini-games are opened in the Gaming edition; only the name of the decade, game or mini-game is sent. No cookies are set, nothing is stored on your device, and no identifier is sent along.

To count visitors per day, the server creates a one-way hash from your IP address, the browser identifier (user agent), the name of the app and a random value that only exists for one day. This value goes into a counter (HyperLogLog) that only knows the number of different visitors and doesn’t store the value itself. Your IP address and browser identifier are not stored. The random value is deleted after two days at the latest; after that, no one, not even the operator, can link a visit to a person or to another day. Only numbers are kept: app launches, visitors and device type per day and app, and how often a decade, game or mini-game was opened, without any link to a visitor.

The numbers are kept in a database of the Upstash service (Upstash, Inc., USA, servers in the EU), which we use through Vercel; their data processing agreement with the EU Standard Contractual Clauses applies. Only the operator can see the numbers, after signing in with his Google account. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in knowing whether and how much RetroMind is used (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); your device is not accessed in the process (Section 25 TDDDG does not apply). You can object (Art. 21 GDPR); an informal message is enough.

11. Feedback

Via “Feedback” (envelope icon at the top of the start page, also in the Gaming edition) you can send us a message. We receive your message, the category you chose and, only if you provide it, your email address for a reply. The message is sent by email to the operator through the Resend service (Resend Inc., 2261 Market Street #5039, San Francisco, CA 94114, USA). In addition, the message text is stored without your email address in the file feedback.md in RetroMind’s public code repository on GitHub (GitHub Inc., USA), where everyone can see it. So please don’t write any personal data in the text. The legal basis is your consent by sending it (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). We delete the email with your address once the matter is settled; we remove an entry in feedback.md on request. For transfers to the USA, the EU Standard Contractual Clauses or the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework apply.

You have the right to

  • data portability (Art. 20),
  • object to processing based on legitimate interests (Art. 21),
  • withdraw consent with effect for the future (Art. 7(3)), in RetroMind directly in the settings,
  • lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority (Art. 77), for example the authority where you live.

For any request, an informal message to the contact address in the Legal Notice is enough.
