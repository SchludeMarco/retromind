Privacy Policy

Applies to RetroMind – Time Travel (retromind.vercel.app) and RetroMind – Gaming (retromind-gaming.vercel.app). Last updated: October 17, 2026

In short

  • RetroMind is a private offer for invited guests only. For this you sign in once with Google, and RetroMind sets a single, technically necessary cookie (see section 14).
  • No tracking, no third-party analytics tools, no ads. The only thing counted is, anonymously, how often the apps are opened (see section 10).
  • Your journey, your profile and your settings stay in your browser on your device.
  • Fonts are hosted on our own server. Our server fetches pictures, sounds and text from Wikipedia/Wikimedia and Wikidata for you, so your browser doesn’t connect to third-party servers for them.
  • Spotify (music) and YouTube (videos) only load once you agree. You only use Google sign-in, AI features and feedback if you trigger them yourself.
  • Under “Your data” / “My data” in the settings you can download everything RetroMind stored about you as a file, or delete all of it with one tap.

1. Controller

The controller within the meaning of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is the operator named in the Legal Notice. There you’ll also find the contact details for any questions about privacy.

2. Hosting and server log files

RetroMind is hosted by Vercel Inc., 440 N Barranca Ave #4133, Covina, CA 91723, USA. When you open the site, Vercel processes technically necessary data: IP address, date and time, the address requested, browser and operating system (user agent), and the amount of data transferred. This is necessary to deliver the site and operate it securely (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Vercel only keeps these logs for a short time. Vercel is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework; in addition, the EU Standard Contractual Clauses apply (Vercel’s Data Processing Addendum).

3. Storage in your browser

RetroMind stores what you enter in your browser’s local storage (localStorage), not on our server: in Time Travel, for example, your name, date of birth, interests, memories, photos and diary; in the Gaming edition, your high score, collection, achievements, coins and found Easter eggs; and in both, settings such as sound, text size, color scheme and your privacy choices. The language you choose is also stored locally in your browser. This storage is strictly necessary for the features you ask for (Section 25(2) No. 2 of the German Telecommunications Digital Services Data Protection Act (TDDDG), Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR). The data stays until you delete it. In both editions the settings (⚙️) have two buttons under “Your data” / “My data”: one downloads everything RetroMind stored in your browser as a file (access and data portability), the other deletes all of it from this device. In Time Travel you can also delete your journey via “Start over” (when signed in with Google, the backup in your Drive too), or clear the site data in your browser. Time Travel and the Gaming edition behave like one app: when you follow a link from one to the other, the link takes this data along in the part of the address after “#” (that part never reaches a server), so both are up to date. Spotify sign-ins and your privacy choices stay on their own address. The Gaming edition’s retro console (an emulator we serve ourselves) also keeps its settings, saves, the emulator files and recently played game files only in your browser (localStorage and IndexedDB). A game file you pick on your device is not sent to our server or to anyone else. The delete button under “My data” removes this data too.

4. AI features (Google Gemini)

Questions, answers, the chat buddy, photo analysis, the hero search and short videos in Time Travel, as well as the Retro Guru and the game guides in the Gaming edition, are generated by Google’s Gemini AI. Only when you use one of these features does our server send the content needed for it to Google’s Gemini API (Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland; processing also by Google LLC, USA): that is, your text and answers, a photo you upload for it, and details such as the decade, search words or game title. Your browser doesn’t connect to Google itself, and your IP address is not passed on. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (the feature you requested). For game guides, Gemini uses Google Search; the search suggestions shown only lead to Google when you click them. Google LLC is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework. Please don’t enter sensitive data about other people into the AI. Answers, texts and videos from the AI are labeled as AI in the app and can contain mistakes. Google only allows the AI features for people aged 18 and over; in Time Travel they therefore stay off when the date of birth you entered makes you younger than 18.

5. Sign-in with Google and backup to Google Drive (optional)

You can choose to sign in with your Google account to back up your journey or your save game to your own Google Drive. Your browser only loads Google’s sign-in script (Google Identity Services) once you reach for the sign-in button. After you sign in, we receive your name, email address, profile picture and, if you allow it, your date of birth and gender from Google. Time Travel only uses this information so it doesn’t have to ask you for it separately; it stays on your device (and in your own Drive backup) and can be changed in the settings at any time. The backup is kept in a private app folder in your Google Drive that only RetroMind can see; none of it is stored on our server. While you are signed in, RetroMind uses it to sync everything it stores in your browser (except sign-in tokens and privacy choices), so Time Travel, the Gaming edition and all your devices share the same data. A sign-in token stays in your browser for no more than one hour. The legal basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, Section 25(1) TDDDG). You can sign out at any time and revoke access in your Google account under “Third-party apps and services”. Provider: Google Ireland Limited (address see above).

6. Music from Spotify (Time Travel and Gaming edition, only with consent)

Time Travel can play the hits of each decade through Spotify’s embedded player (Spotify AB, Regeringsgatan 19, 111 53 Stockholm, Sweden), and the Gaming edition can play a playlist of ’80s heavy metal in the arcade. The player only loads once you choose “Allow music” (Time Travel) or “Allow metal” (Gaming edition); each address asks separately. Spotify then receives, among other things, your IP address and device information and may set cookies, including to recognize you if you’re signed in to Spotify. Optionally, you can also sign in with your Spotify account (Spotify then receives profile data and your email; we only store a sign-in token in your browser). Signed in with Spotify Premium, you hear a short clip of the song playing inside in front of the gaming hall; our server fetches this clip from Spotify (or, if there is none, from Deezer), your browser doesn’t contact these services for it, and nothing but the song is sent. With Premium, the Gaming edition also plays the music directly in your browser through Spotify’s Web Playback SDK: for this your browser loads Spotify’s player (sdk.scdn.co) and signs in to Spotify with your sign-in token, so Spotify sees which songs you listen to. Which songs are on a playlist is asked of Spotify by our server, without any data about you. In Time Travel, a short clip of that year’s hit plays when you arrive in a year, but only if you chose “Allow music”. Our server fetches this clip too (from Deezer); only the song’s title and artist are sent, nothing about you. The legal basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, Section 25(1) TDDDG). You can withdraw it at any time in the settings (⚙️) (“Stop loading Spotify”). Spotify’s privacy policy: spotify.com/de/legal/privacy-policy.

7. Videos from YouTube (Time Travel and Gaming edition, only with consent)

After every journey, Time Travel shows a year in review from YouTube under “Welcome to the year …”, and the Gaming edition shows matching videos for a game (YouTube is run by Google Ireland Limited, address see above). Our server asks YouTube which videos match; only the search words are sent for this (the year or the game title), and our server also fetches the thumbnails. The video player itself only loads once you choose “Allow YouTube”. We use the privacy-enhanced mode (youtube-nocookie.com). Even so, YouTube receives your IP address and device information when a video plays and may store data in your browser. The legal basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, Section 25(1) TDDDG); you can withdraw it at any time in the settings under “Privacy”. Google’s privacy policy: policies.google.com/privacy.

8. Content from Wikipedia, weather and other pictures and sounds

Our server loads text and pictures from Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons, game data from Wikidata, YouTube thumbnails, sound effects and background patterns from the respective source and passes them on to you. Your browser has no contact with these providers, and our server doesn’t pass on any data about you. Fonts are built directly into RetroMind; there is no connection to Google Fonts.

Heroes of your youth (Time Travel): when you search for a hero there, our server sends your search words to Wikipedia to find matching articles and, if you type a description, to Gemini (see section 4). Only the search words reach Wikipedia, not your IP address; your name, birth date and memories are not sent. Photos on the hero pages come only from Wikimedia Commons under free licenses; the author, license and source are shown below each one. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (the search you requested).

Weather in the living-room window (Time Travel, only if you switch it on): if you choose to see your real weather in the window of the drawn living room, you either give a town or let your browser share your location (your browser asks you first). The location is rounded to about 10 km and stored only in your browser. To show the weather, our server asks the free weather service Open-Meteo (Open-Meteo.com, Switzerland) for the current weather at these rounded coordinates, or looks up the town you typed. Only the coordinates or the town name reach Open-Meteo, not your IP address. Legal basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR); you can switch it off at any time under the living room with “Turn off”, which also deletes the stored place.

9. Links to other sites

RetroMind links to other sites (e.g. Wikipedia, YouTube, Spotify, GameFAQs, MobyGames, Reddit, archive.org). Only when you click such a link does your browser connect to the respective provider; that provider’s privacy policy applies there.

10. Anonymous usage statistics

So that the operator can see how many people use RetroMind, our server counts how often Time Travel and the Gaming edition are opened. For this, the app sends the name of the app (“zeitreise” or “gaming”), the type of device (phone, tablet or computer, derived from the browser identifier) and whether the app is running as an installed app to our server on launch. It also counts which decades are opened in Time Travel and which games and mini-games are opened in the Gaming edition; only the name of the decade, game or mini-game is sent. No cookies are set, nothing is stored on your device, and no identifier is sent along.

To count visitors per day, the server creates a one-way hash from your IP address, the browser identifier (user agent), the name of the app and a random value that only exists for one day. This value goes into a counter (HyperLogLog) that only knows the number of different visitors and doesn’t store the value itself. Your IP address and browser identifier are not stored. The random value is deleted after two days at the latest; after that, no one, not even the operator, can link a visit to a person or to another day. Only numbers are kept: app launches, visitors and device type per day and app, and how often a decade, game or mini-game was opened, without any link to a visitor.

The numbers are kept in a database of the Upstash service (Upstash, Inc., USA, servers in the EU), which we use through Vercel; their data processing agreement with the EU Standard Contractual Clauses applies. Only the operator can see the numbers, after signing in with his Google account. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in knowing whether and how much RetroMind is used (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); your device is not accessed in the process (Section 25 TDDDG does not apply). You can object (Art. 21 GDPR); an informal message is enough.

11. High score table (Gaming edition)

After a good round in a points mini-game you can put yourself on the high score table with three letters or digits, like at an arcade cabinet. Only when you tap “OK” does the app send the game, your three characters and your score to our server. It stores them in the same Upstash database as the statistics (see section 10); the best 50 entries per game are kept, and anyone who opens the game sees the top 10. Nothing else is stored, in particular no IP address and no identifier. So please don’t pick letters that make you recognizable to others if you don’t want that. The app also remembers your last initials and its own table in your browser. The legal basis is your consent by entering them (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR); we remove an entry on request.

12. Feedback

Via “Feedback” (envelope icon at the bottom left of the Time Travel start page, also in the Gaming edition) you can send us a message. We receive your message, the category you chose and, only if you provide it, your email address for a reply. The message is sent by email to the operator through the Resend service (Resend Inc., 2261 Market Street #5039, San Francisco, CA 94114, USA). In addition, the message text is stored without your email address in the file feedback.md in RetroMind’s public code repository on GitHub (GitHub Inc., USA), where everyone can see it. So please don’t write any personal data in the text. The legal basis is your consent by sending it (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). We delete the email with your address once the matter is settled; we remove an entry in feedback.md on request. For transfers to the USA, the EU Standard Contractual Clauses or the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework apply.

14. Access for invited guests only

RetroMind is not a public offer; it only opens for people the operator invited. On your first visit you sign in with your Google account on the guest page; only when you point at or tap the button does your browser load Google’s sign-in script for this. Our server checks whether your email address is on the guest list. The same sign-in also counts as the Google sign-in in Time Travel and the Gaming edition (section 5): Google asks for the permissions described there right away (backup in your own Drive, profile, optionally birthday and gender), which you can untick in Google’s window, and the sign-in token stays in your browser for at most an hour as described there. The guest list holds the email addresses of invited guests and is stored in the Upstash database (servers in the EU, see section 10); only the operator adds addresses.

If you are on the list, RetroMind sets the cookie rm_guest. It contains your email address, the time of the check and a signature so nobody can forge it, and scripts cannot read it. It is valid for 30 days and, when you visit, is checked against the guest list at most once a day and then renewed. This cookie is strictly necessary so that only guests can open RetroMind (Section 25(2) No. 2 TDDDG). The legal basis for the check is our legitimate interest in keeping RetroMind private (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR), and for the guest list the invitation you accepted (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). If you no longer want to be a guest, a message is enough and your address is removed from the list. This page, the legal notice and the guest page can be opened without signing in.

Time Travel and the Gaming edition live on two addresses that don’t share cookies. When you as a guest follow a link from one to the other, our server stores your email address under a random one-time code in the Upstash database for at most two minutes; the other address redeems the code, sets its own rm_guest cookie, and the code is deleted. If you are signed in with Google, the link also carries your sign-in token, in the part of the address after the #, which is never sent to a server; the other edition stores it in your browser and removes it from the address right away. That way you only sign in once.
